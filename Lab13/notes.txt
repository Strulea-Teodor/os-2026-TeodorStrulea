Lab 13 - Semaphores in Your Own Kernel - Observe answers (Part 3, mix)

1. Without the lock the letters mix because one line costs 31 system calls
   (30 letters + newline, one write() each). The console keeps each single
   write whole, but between any two writes the other process, running on
   another CPU, can slip in its own write. Nothing keeps the 31 writes of
   one line together.

2. The two lines of mix.c that make one line a critical section are
   sem_down(3) before the inner for loop and sem_up(3) after it.
   Semaphore 3 starts at 1: the first process takes the unit, the other
   sleeps in the kernel until the first has written its 31 bytes and ups.

3. "while (s->value == 0)" and not "if": a process can wake up and still
   find 0. wakeup() makes RUNNABLE every process sleeping on the address;
   if two were waiting and sem_up added one unit, both wake, the first to
   run takes the unit (value back to 0) and the second finds 0 again.
   With "if" it would decrement to -1 and pass; with "while" it sleeps
   again. The same loop re-tests after a wakeup caused by kill.

4. release(&s->lock) BEFORE sleep(): a process must never sleep holding a
   spinlock. acquire disables interrupts and other CPUs spin on the lock.
   A sleeping owner cannot run until sem_up wakes it, but sem_up on another
   CPU would spin forever on the lock the sleeper holds -> the kernel hangs
   (or panics in sched(), which checks that no other lock is held). The
   two-step sleep_prepare/sleep exists so the lock can be released between
   registering the channel and sleeping without losing the wakeup.

Optional: sem_trydown (27) implemented in kernel/sem.c; user/trytest.c
shows 0 (unit taken), -1 (nothing left, no sleep), 0 again after sem_up.
